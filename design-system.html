FocusFlow Design System — v0.1 proposal

A visual direction for the screens, built against the constraints already fixed in the PRD and technical design: mobile-first, one phone screen with no sideways scrolling, a dense day timeline, and WCAG AA. Every value here is derived from an enum in the schema rather than invented, so the interface cannot drift away from the data.

Preview — not yet Tailwind WCAG AA target Colour never alone 390px first

1. Four rules the whole system obeys

These decide arguments later. When two options look equally nice, the rule wins.

Colour never carries meaning alone

Every state has a shape, a word, or a glyph as well as a colour. Someone who cannot distinguish the colours still reads the screen correctly. Required by the PRD, and it is also just better UI.

16px body text, 44px touch targets

Body text never drops below 16px on mobile. Anything tappable is at least 44px tall. Times and numbers use tabular figures so digits line up down a timeline.

Fixed things look fixed

A meeting has a solid heavy edge and a lock glyph. A break has a dashed edge. You should be able to tell what can be moved by looking at the shape before reading a single word.

Quiet by default, loud only when it helps

Most of the day view is calm greys and navy. Colour is spent on the two things that need a decision: where a task landed, and what did not fit.

This scale is deliberately one colour, on purpose. It runs dark to light by intensity, not green-to-red by goodness. A rough day is not a failed day, and the PRD's tone rules ban red, "you failed", and any count of missed days — a red mood chip would break all three at once. Five points rather than four so there is a true middle: an even scale pushes people toward an answer they do not feel. `ROUGH` is the wording that survives that rule.

4. Space, radius, elevation

A 4px base scale. If a gap is not on this list, it is a mistake.

Spacing

4812 162024 3240

16 is the default gap. 12 inside a component. 24 between sections.

Radius

6 sm 10 md 14 lg full

Chips are pills. Cards and inputs are 10. Sheets are 14.

Elevation

0 flat 1 card 2 sheet

Three levels, no more. Shadows mean "this floats above the page".

Why a task is where it is — enum BindingConstraint

These are the seven codes PlacementReason can hold. Each gets a plain-English label, because the stored value is a code and the person needs a sentence.

⚡ Energy match ⏳ Deadline ◷ Working hours ⊗ Clashes with a block ↑ Top priority ✋ You moved it ⚠ Did not fit
Only one of these is allowed to feel urgent. "Did not fit" is amber because it is the one state the user must act on. If everything were amber, nothing would be.

Reading the shapes

Solid heavy edge = a task you can drag. Lock glyph and plain edge = fixed. Double edge = your own personal time. Dashed = a break the app suggested.

Times on the left

Hours run down the margin, never repeated inside a block. One glance down the column tells you the shape of the day, and blocks never have to print their own start time.

Failures go last

"Did not fit" sits below the timeline, not inside it. The day you achieved stays clean; the things needing a decision are collected in one place at the end.

7. Explaining the plan — the part that makes this product different

The schema stores facts, not sentences: an energy level, a constraint code, a list of what it competed against. This card is where those facts become one readable line. It is generated from the fields, never typed by hand.

Why is this here?

Placed at 09:00 because your energy is high and it is the hardest task you have.
▲▲ high ⚡ energy_match ↑ difficulty hard

Why did that move?

Moved from 11:00 to 15:30 because Design review was added at 10:00 and would have overlapped.
⊗ block_collision 🔒 Design review
Why the sentence is assembled, not stored. Storing "Placed at 09:00 because your energy is high" freezes today's wording. When the product grows, those same stored facts answer questions nobody has asked yet — and you cannot query a sentence.

Empty states — required by Day 8, easy to forget

Four blanks the app can genuinely hit on day one. Each says what is missing and gives the one action that fixes it. A blank screen with no button is a dead end.

○

No tasks yet

Add your first task and the scheduler will find it a home.

◷

Nothing planned

Your day is empty. Re-plan to place whatever you have waiting.

✓

All done

Every task placed today is finished. Nothing is waiting on you.

⚡

No energy curve yet

Draw your curve once and scheduling gets noticeably better.

Note the button on every one. The technical design originally listed empty states as Day 9 work. They have since been moved forward to Day 8, because by the last build day they are the easiest thing to skip — and a first-run tester who meets four blank panels concludes the app is broken.

9. Declared versus observed energy

This section exists because you accepted the second Phase 0 recommendation: the prototype uses a curve the person drew themselves, and observed data arrives later. The design has to make that visible from the start, so nobody is later surprised by what the app started inferring.

✎ Declared — you drew this

The energy strip on the day view carries this label whenever the curve came from setup. The app is using your judgement, not a measurement.

◎ Observed — from your check-ins future

When one-tap energy logging ships, this label replaces the declared one, and the design already has a place for it. Nothing about the layout changes.

The rule this enforces. The app may never present an inferred energy level as if the person told it. The source is always on screen. The schema already carries EnergySource { DECLARED OBSERVED } — this is where that column becomes visible.

11. What I need you to decide about this

These are judgement calls about taste and scope, not technical ones. Each is cheap to change now and expensive later.
