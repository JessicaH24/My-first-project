Purpose of This Document

This document defines what FocusFlow must do, for whom, and how success will be judged. It specifies the experience and behaviour of the product across the full user lifecycle. It is the shared contract between product, design, engineering, and quality.

Technical architecture, infrastructure, data modelling, and engineering implementation are explicitly out of scope and are documented separately in the technical design specification.

How to read the requirement tables. Every functional requirement carries a unique identifier (for example S4) and a priority of Must, Should, or Could. Must requirements define the release. Should requirements are strongly desirable and may be descoped with documented agreement. Could requirements are candidates for the next release. Identifiers are referenced by the traceability matrix in Appendix B and are used to raise defects during quality assurance.

1. Executive Summary

Busy professionals do not have a planning problem. They have a coherence problem.

They already own calendars, to-do lists, habit trackers, and journals. What they lack is a single system in which their ambitions, their hours, and their reflections belong to the same structure. Instead they have four disconnected tools that disagree with one another, none of which are aware of their energy levels, and none of which help them notice when they are working hard on the wrong thing.

The consequence is a specific and recognisable failure pattern. A professional plans a realistic day, has it fragmented by meetings, finishes most of the important work but cannot remember which parts advanced which ambition, and closes the day with no record of what actually happened. The next morning begins with reconstruction rather than intention. Within three weeks, the tools are still installed and unused.

FocusFlow is a single application that closes this loop end to end. A user defines their goals, captures everything that crosses their mind without breaking focus, and receives a realistic, energy-aware plan for each day. They work inside protected focus blocks, reflect honestly in the evening on what happened and why, and receive a revised plan that adjusts without blame. Once a week, the app tells them plainly whether they are on track and what should change.

Three choices define the product:

  • Goals are the spine. Every task traces to a goal. If it does not, the system asks whether it should exist.
  • Plans account for energy, not just hours. Difficult work is placed where the user is sharp. Most competing schedulers treat a 40-minute report and a 5-minute email as equivalent.
  • Failure is designed for. Missed days are normal input, not failure. The system replans and stays silent about streaks and shame.

Success for V1 is measured by whether users return daily and whether their goals advance. A user who abandons the app after a bad week, or who completes tasks that do not serve their ambitions, has not been served â€” regardless of what the engagement dashboards report.

2. Problem and Opportunity

2.1 The Five Failure Points

#FailureDescriptionConsequence
1Disconnected from ambitionTask lists record activity without purpose. Nothing links a 40-item list to whether a career or health ambition is moving.Busy, exhausted, and no closer to the thing that mattered.
2Time planned on a flat canvasCalendars treat all work as equivalent regardless of cognitive demand. Energy, attention, and task type are ignored.Deep work is scheduled at 4pm and lost to fatigue.
3Planning is manual and repetitiveRebuilding tomorrow every evening is unpaid labour. Most users abandon it within days.Days become reactive rather than intentional.
4Reflection is absentNo tool asks what worked, what blocked, or whether the plan is still right. The system collects data and never interprets it.Repeats the same mistakes indefinitely.
5Failure is punishedStreaks break, backlogs swell, overdue items accumulate in red. The app becomes a record of guilt.One bad week triggers abandonment at the point where support is most needed.

2.2 Why Existing Approaches Fall Short

CategoryStrengthStructural gap
To-do listsFast capture, low friction, clear completionNo time protection, no goal linkage, no reflection. A list is a record of intention, not of achievement.
CalendarsTime is real and finite; meetings are unavoidableUnaware of goals and energy. Deep and shallow work are indistinguishable to it.
Habit trackersStreak mechanics drive consistencyTracks behaviour in isolation. Creates a second parallel system competing with the planner for attention.
Journaling appsSupports reflection and emotional processingDisconnected from the day's work, so reflection rarely converts into a change in plan.
Task managers with goalsLink intent to activityGoals become static labels. No scheduling intelligence, so the link changes nothing about how the day runs.
The opportunity. No incumbent spans goal definition through daily scheduling, execution protection, honest reflection, and weekly course correction in one coherent loop. Each of these exists, but on different surfaces with different assumptions. The gap is not a missing feature. It is the missing thread.

2.3 The Moment of Abandonment

Abandonment is rarely caused by lack of features. It occurs at a predictable point: the first difficult week, when the backlog grows, the daily plan slips, and the app's accumulated signals turn punitive at exactly the moment the user needs encouragement. Every requirement in Section 11 that governs failure behaviour exists to prevent this specific outcome.

4. Product Principles

These are decision criteria. When requirements conflict, these principles resolve the conflict without escalation.

#PrincipleMeaning and application
1Goals are the spineNothing exists without a reason. Every task, project, and routine traces to a goal or a declared life obligation. A feature that cannot justify its existence against a goal does not ship.
2Protect attention, not just timeFriction in capture and planning is the real enemy, not a shortage of hours. Any flow that takes more than a few seconds to dismiss itself as friction.
3Plan for the human, not the idealSchedules respect energy, boundaries, and history. An impossible plan is worse than no plan, because it teaches the user the app is fiction.
4Forgiving by designPlans will break. A missed task is normal input to be re-planned, never a failure to be recorded. No red overdue walls, no streak shaming.
5Reflection is part of the workA plan without reflection produces motion, not progress. Review lives in the daily flow rather than depending on motivation.
6Right or silentThe coach speaks when it has something useful to say and stays quiet otherwise. Silence is a designed feature, not a missing capability.
7Show the reasoningEvery automated suggestion explains itself. Users must be able to see why, and disagree. A suggestion that cannot be justified is not made.
8Small surface, deep habitFive core surfaces used daily beat thirty features used once. Depth of use beats breadth of features.

5.4 User Story Summary

The following stories are written from the user's perspective. Detailed acceptance criteria appear in Section 9.

  • As a busy professional, I want my tasks connected to real goals, so that I can see my effort is going somewhere.
  • As someone constantly interrupted, I want to capture a thought in under five seconds, so that I never lose it or lose my current focus.
  • As a person who is tired at 4pm, I want hard work scheduled in my sharp hours, so that I actually finish what matters.
  • As a user whose day fell apart, I want a new plan without a lecture, so that I start again tomorrow instead of quitting.
  • As a reflective person, I want prompts about what actually went well, so that I stop repeating the same mistakes.
  • As an ambitious professional, I want to know each week whether I'm on track, so that I can change course before months are wasted.
  • As someone who values privacy, I want my data to be mine, so that I can trust the app with my real goals and thoughts.

7.3 Explicit Design Rejections

These are recorded because they are common in this product category and are being deliberately declined. Each represents a tradeoff accepted in exchange for user trust.

RejectedReason
A separate habit trackerCreates a second parallel system competing with the planner. The target user is time-poor; a second place to record things is a cost, not a feature.
Points, badges, achievement unlockablesConverts meaningful professional work into a game. It devalues the goals being pursued and attracts the wrong user.
Guilt-inducing languageNo red overdue walls, no "you failed" messaging, no punitive streak resets, no missed-day counts. Principled rejection under Principle 4.
A real-name leaderboardPublic ranking of personal productivity data invites comparison that damages rather than motivates the target user. The leaderboard uses display names and is opt-in only.
Streak-based pressure on the daily ritualsThe morning and evening rituals are the habit worth building. Attaching a streak to them punishes exactly the lapses that most need forgiveness. Streaks apply to routines only.

13. AI Behaviour and Guardrails

AI is used for parsing, scheduling, and coaching. Because incorrect output would erode trust faster than no output at all, the following constraints are binding.

13.1 Permitted Uses

UseScope and Constraint
Capture parsingExtracting action, time, date, and priority from natural input. Low stakes, fully user-correctable before filing.
SchedulingArranging existing user-approved tasks. Operates only on data the user has supplied and constraints the user has set.
Journal promptsAsking questions about the user's own stated experience.
Pattern detectionIdentifying statistical tendencies in the user's own recorded behaviour, with thresholds disclosed.
Task breakdownProposing next actions, always presented for approval and never created automatically.

13.2 Prohibited Uses

  • No diagnosis. The product does not assess mental health, diagnose conditions, or present itself as a wellbeing authority. It records what users report and reflects patterns back.
  • No manipulation. No emotional pressure, guilt framing, or urgency manufactured to drive engagement.
  • No fabricated data. Insights require real observations. Where data is insufficient, the product says so and states what is needed.
  • No unrequested generation. The coach does not create goals, tasks, or commitments without explicit user action.
  • No inference stated as fact. "You seem to avoid this project" is acceptable. "You are avoiding this project" is not.
  • No decisions on the user's behalf. The system proposes; the user disposes.
  • No manipulation of the leaderboard. The coach may suggest resting, but never suggests increasing activity to improve a rank.

13.3 Disclosure Requirements

  • Every AI-generated element is visibly attributable.
  • The basis for any inference is stated alongside it.
  • Users can correct any AI output, and the correction is retained for future output.
  • AI features not essential to core function can be disabled without degrading the daily loop.
  • Data used for AI processing is disclosed in plain language at the point of use, not buried in terms.

15. Measurement Plan

This section defines what is measured and why. It does not specify instrumentation, which is a technical concern.

15.1 Measurement Principles

  • Outcomes over activity. Goal progression and retention are primary. Task counts and session lengths are diagnostic only.

Weekly reviewThe once-weekly assessment of progress, patterns, and direction, ending in course-correction decisions.
Course correctionThe explicit decision to continue, adjust, or drop a goal based on evidence.
ReplanA proposed revised plan following incomplete work, presented for user acceptance rather than imposed.
Focus sessionA protected period of work with notifications silenced and distracting applications blocked.
Consistency scoreA weekly measure derived from completed against uncompleted tasks. Defined in Section 9.9, I6.
InsightA pattern the system surfaces from the user's own recorded behaviour, with its basis stated.
CoachThe product's proactive assistant. Speaks only when it has something useful to say.
Meaningful goal progressionThe North Star measure: completing work that materially advances an active goal.
Must / Should / CouldRequirement priorities. Must defines V1, Should is desirable and descopable, Could is a V2 candidate.
